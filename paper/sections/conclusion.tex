\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formulated bird's-eye disaster VLN as joint language-guided navigation and evidence-aware damage judgment, and introduced a calibrated active-reinspection mechanism for this setting.
Under event-disjoint training, temperature scaling improves in-domain ECE but not held-out ECE, and difference attention no longer shows a large holdout accuracy advantage.
An event-disjoint localization U-Net replaces a weak YOLO proposer and improves building proposal quality, while damage probabilities and calibration remain those of the dual-temporal classifier.
In the strict U-Net-proposer E1 and E11 suites, every configuration obtains
zero strict success, and no episode yields damage evidence or a reinspection
trigger.
The proposal improvement therefore does not establish navigation or judgment
gains, and the controller cannot be compared when the upstream stack never
exercises it.
Thus the correct conclusion is not that reinspection already improves mission success.
Rather, \ProjectName provides an executable hypothesis and an auditable protocol: when uncertainty is calibrated, can a UAV spend a fixed sensing budget to obtain more reliable disaster judgments?
We answer the perception-calibration half of that question, relieve the proposer bottleneck without changing the claim contract, and report that the action-selection half remains open under the frozen evaluation.
