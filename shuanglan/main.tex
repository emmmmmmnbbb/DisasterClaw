\title{DisasterClaw: A georeferenced platform and task benchmark for closed-loop UAV disaster inspection}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\affiliation{organization={Anonymous submission}}
\begin{abstract}
Aerial disaster inspection requires linking sensing actions to evidence and task outcomes, yet disaster datasets are usually organized as static image records. We present DisasterClaw, a georeferenced platform and task benchmark that transforms paired xBD imagery into map-anchored interactive scenes for closed-loop UAV inspection. Disaster imagery, building annotations, task regions, and vehicle states share a geographic reference. Presence, damage, count, and spatial tasks are generated programmatically, with reference answers computed from registered annotations and geometry. Automated checks and independent review by two annotators yield 160 final tasks over 44 regions in three disaster events. An interactive console and a headless runner support traceable observation--perception--action--answer episodes. A development camera-ladder diagnostic shows that narrower footprints can both correct and harm building predictions. Seven frozen policies evaluated over three generation repeats produce 3,360 episodes. The task-conditioned policy attains 75.00\% accuracy, compared with 72.29\% for no reobservation, and executes 161 reobservations compared with 923 for always reobserving. The observed gain over holding is 2.71 percentage points, but the prespecified ROI-cluster, multiplicity-adjusted interval spans $-2.04$ to $+7.50$ points. Thus, a stable advantage over holding is not established. The contribution is a platform and benchmark for measuring task-dependent observation value, bounded by rendered orthographic imagery, simplified motion, prior xBD exposure of the fixed ChangeOS backbone, and three evaluation events.
\end{abstract}
