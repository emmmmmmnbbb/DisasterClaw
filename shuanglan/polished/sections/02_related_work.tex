\section{Related work}
\subsection{Aerial interaction and embodied evaluation}
AerialVLN, CityNav, and OpenFly emphasize language-conditioned aerial navigation, while AeroVerse and BEDI broaden evaluation toward perception, spatial reasoning, planning, motion, and perception--decision--action loops~\cite{liu2023aerialvln,lee2025citynav,gao2026openfly,yao2024aeroverse,guo2026bedi}. CityNavAgent uses hierarchical semantic planning and trajectory memory for urban aerial navigation, and the ICLR 2025 UAV platform and benchmark work combines realistic flight simulation with target-oriented navigation tasks~\cite{zhang2025citynavagent,wang2025openuav}. AerialClaw, a recent preprint, connects language-specified missions to executable UAV skills, runtime feedback, and simulation~\cite{li2026aerialclaw}. Together, these systems evaluate action-conditioned observations and broader aerial mission behavior. Aerial simulators such as AirSim and Flightmare couple sensing to vehicle dynamics and 3D rendering~\cite{shah2018airsim,song2021flightmare}. DisasterClaw addresses a complementary question: whether a particular observation change helps a fixed geographic task, with the answer-level effect recorded separately from an extra inference call.

\subsection{Disaster and remote-sensing agents}
xBD supplies paired disaster imagery and building-damage annotations~\cite{gupta2019xbd}. RescueADI, ThinkGeo, and DisasterM3 study tool use or multimodal disaster interpretation from remote-sensing records~\cite{liu2024rescueadi,thinkgeo2025,wang2025disasterm3}. These resources motivate disaster-specific questions. Evaluating an answer from a static image, however, does not measure the value of an agent's decision to observe again. DisasterClaw binds question scope, vehicle state, rendered observation, and terminal answer in one record. This binding also exposes a failure mode: a view that improves one building prediction can remove evidence required by a count or spatial question.

Table~\ref{tab:capability-comparison} summarizes the evaluation capabilities most relevant to this distinction. Among the compared benchmarks, DisasterClaw explicitly standardizes a fixed annotation-derived geographic task and answer under view change, geographic evidence traces across observations, and a matched fixed-view answer call. These features define its contribution within a controlled observation setting.

\subsection{Task value of an observation}
Active perception treats sensing as a task-directed action, from foundational active-vision formulations to learned sequential localization policies~\cite{bajcsy1988active,aloimonos1988active,caicedo2015active}. In an embodied system, uncertainty or action feasibility can inform a sensing decision, but predictive uncertainty alone does not measure whether the subsequent task answer is correct~\cite{ren2023knowno,guo2017calibration,ovadia2019can}. We therefore report localization, answer corrections and harms, executed rechecks, and terminal accuracy separately. The task-conditioned rule in this paper is a fixed heuristic for descending subject to predicted ROI coverage and motion cost. Complete-policy comparisons measure outcomes at the realized operating points. Fixed-view matched calls at executed recheck transitions control for an additional answer opportunity; the MESSI repeat-high arm further compares a real new view with a second inference on the same view.
