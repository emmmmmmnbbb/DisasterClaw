\section{Results and discussion}

\subsection{Image-aware performance and shortcut controls}
The final answer distribution yields a strong task-majority baseline: the development-fitted task-majority rule attains 57.50\% (92/160). This result reflects the frozen answer distribution, particularly for count questions, without using visual evidence. The text-only S1\_LANGUAGE control reaches 28.75\% over 480 question--repeat instances, and the permitted-metadata S2\_METADATA control reaches 36.25\% (Table~\ref{tab:shortcut-controls}). Neither control receives imagery or detector evidence. In contrast, the image-aware A0\_NO\_RECHECK and T1\_TASK operating points reach 72.29\% and 75.00\%, respectively. Their paired gaps to S1 are $+43.54$ and $+46.25$ points, and to S2 are $+36.04$ and $+38.75$ points; all four 95\% ROI-cluster intervals exclude zero. The complete image-aware pipelines therefore outperform the evaluated controls based on question wording and permitted non-image metadata. Because S1/S2 also use a modality-specific prompt and omit the hybrid rule path, the comparison measures a pipeline-level difference and does not isolate the causal effect of image access.

\subsection{Observation changes under matched answer calls}
The policy comparison alone cannot distinguish a new image from another answer opportunity. We therefore created paired branches at all 161 executed T1 rechecks, with each pair sharing its pre-recheck state. The fixed branch reran perception and answering on the previous view. The changed branch executed the original recheck before perception and answering. The strict validity gate accepted all 161 pairs, with no missing pair or execution error. The branch schedule reproduced the original recheck-step locations in 118 of 119 episodes; the one mismatch retained the same two rechecks but placed the second at a different step, reflecting re-runnable perception-path variation.

The changed-view branch produces correct candidate answers in 96/161 transitions (59.63\%), versus 81/161 (50.31\%) for the fixed-view matched call, a paired difference of $+9.32$ points (95\% ROI-cluster interval $[-2.00,+21.74]$). The point estimate is accompanied by 36 fixed-wrong/changed-correct transitions and 21 fixed-correct/changed-wrong transitions; 44 pairs are wrong in both branches and 60 correct in both. Answers disagree in 42.24\% of pairs. Thus the changed observation can alter the answer path beyond a repeated call on the old image, but the clustered interval does not establish a stable positive average effect in the sampled ROIs.

Excluding the one episode whose rerun placed its second recheck at step 2 rather than reference step 3 removes two wrong--wrong pairs. The remaining 159 pairs from 118 episodes have 81/159 fixed-view and 96/159 changed-view correct candidates, a $+9.43$-point paired difference (95\% ROI-cluster interval $[-2.03,+22.00]$), with the same 36 help and 21 harm transitions. As a separate old-view repeat check, the pre-recheck and fixed-branch image bytes and normalized candidate answers agree in all 161/161 original forks (159/159 after exclusion). The two old-view calls use different step seeds and evidence prompts, however, so this is observed answer stability under identical image bytes rather than a controlled same-prompt, same-seed repeatability estimate.

\subsection{Building-level evidence and task-dependent outcomes}
The camera-ladder diagnostic holds 7,238 annotated buildings in 43 development ROIs fixed while changing the rendered footprint. ChangeOS localization recall rises from 22.09\% at cruise to 34.06\% at intermediate and 56.52\% at floor. With every unmatched ground-truth building counted as incorrect, binary building accuracy rises from 20.01\% to 31.06\% and 53.65\%. Among matched buildings only, binary damage accuracy is 90.56\%, 91.20\%, and 94.92\%, while matched-only macro-F1 is 0.8714, 0.8743, and 0.9232. The paired cruise-to-floor outcomes include 2,696 incorrect-to-correct changes and 261 correct-to-incorrect changes. The building-level diagnostic shows that rendering changes affect the predicted evidence available to the agent. Asymmetric pre-/post-image processing and changes in context prevent attributing these effects solely to GSD or physical descent.

Table~\ref{tab:detection-fp-audit} separates localization from conditional damage classification. Localization precision falls to 68.65\% at floor even as recall and matched-building damage accuracy rise; unmatched predictions increase to 43.44 per ROI. TP/FP/FN use a one-to-one nearest-centroid match within 12~m, so an unmatched prediction can reflect a duplicate, localization error, or missing annotation as well as a spurious building. The all-GT accuracy denominator includes missed buildings but does not penalize extra predictions; matched-only accuracy and macro-F1 exclude misses and unmatched predictions. The gains in damage accuracy and the increase in localization false positives thus describe different aspects of the detection pipeline.

The matched-call results show that task answers also respond differently to observation changes. Damage and count transitions have positive point differences, whereas spatial transitions have a negative point difference (Table~\ref{tab:matched-view}). A more local view can improve the evidence for a marked building or a damaged-count decision, yet it can change the selected target for a nearest-building direction judgment. These task-wise estimates are descriptive because T1 selects the recheck subsets, which contain only 18 count and 20 spatial transitions.

\subsection{Case study: Re-observation using real UAV imagery}
\label{sec:messi-case}
The preceding experiments change observations by resampling orthographic xBD images. We constructed a separate exploratory case with real multi-elevation UAV images from MESSI~\cite{pinkovich2025messi} to ask whether a lower-altitude acquisition adds information beyond a repeat call or digital enlargement of the high image. During pilot preparation, the proposed parked-vehicle question did not provide stable positive regions in the three candidate evaluation sequences. We therefore fixed one static presence question before formal inference: whether a marked ground region contains visible tree or shrub canopy, supported by MESSI's vegetation annotation (class 12) and visual review of both source images. This is a scene-content check, not disaster-damage recognition.

The case contains 12 fixed regions, four each from MESSI sequences \texttt{100\_0002}, \texttt{100\_0003}, and \texttt{100\_0042}, with six positive and six negative answers. Four regions from \texttt{100\_0001} served only as pilot items and were excluded. For each evaluation sequence, we selected the frames nearest 100 and 30~m in the provided height relative to takeoff: the observed high range was 99.9--100.2~m and the low range 29.8--30.3~m. We matched local image features across the two frames, estimated a homography, and visually checked the paired crops and labels before model outputs. These values are relative heights, not calibrated local above-ground distances. Frame selection by height defines an offline review; it does not reconstruct a flown descent decision.

Each item first presented Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with the high image and a red outline of the region. The resulting answer was copied into three isolated second-turn histories. Branch A repeated the same outlined high image. B was an 800$\times$800-pixel digital enlargement of the corresponding crop from the approximately 100-m source image; it magnifies existing pixels and adds no new source detail. C was an 800$\times$800-pixel crop from a separate image acquired at approximately 30~m. B and C show approximately the same ground footprint at matched display dimensions, but they are different source images. All branches used the same question, second prompt, greedy decoding, 16-token output limit, and one second answer call. Ground-truth masks and answers were withheld from prompts. All 12 items completed their planned first and three branch calls; \texttt{UNCERTAIN} and invalid answers counted as incorrect.

The primary paired comparison is C versus B (Table~\ref{tab:messi-case}). C answered 11/12 correctly (91.67\%), versus 10/12 (83.33\%) for B, a descriptive difference of $+8.33$ percentage points. Relative to B, the new image made two incorrect answers correct and one correct answer incorrect. Relative to the first high-image answer, C corrected five items and harmed one. A answered 5/12 (41.67\%), versus 11/12 for C, but this secondary contrast also changes contextual extent. At the sequence level, B and C were tied at 3/4 in \texttt{100\_0002} and 4/4 in \texttt{100\_0003}; C was 4/4 versus B's 3/4 in \texttt{100\_0042}. Thus the aggregate difference is one answer among twelve and should not be read as a stable expected gain.

Figure~\ref{fig:messi-case} shows both directions of the paired outcome. In \texttt{100\_0042\_r16}, the high crop produced \texttt{UNCERTAIN}, whereas the low image correctly rejected canopy on paving. In \texttt{100\_0002\_r02}, the high crop correctly identified vegetation, whereas the low image incorrectly answered \texttt{NO} despite visible canopy at the left of the region. A new view can therefore clarify or lose task-relevant evidence. The sample is small and selected for clear cross-height labels; regions within a sequence are correlated. Registration and axis-aligned high cropping leave some footprint mismatch: the high crop's bounding rectangle exceeds the mapped quadrilateral area by 2.8--15.9\% across the twelve items. Changes in illumination, perspective, and scene context also accompany resolution changes. These fixed-observation branches measure the value of the supplied view; the following replay tests whether an adapted agent requests that view.

\subsubsection{Action-gated offline replay}
We next replayed the same twelve regions through DisasterClaw's \texttt{AgentVqaController} and T1 task-conditioned utility gate. This exploratory follow-up was designed after the fixed-observation case. The offline environment initially exposed only the outlined high image; it released the paired low crop after an authorized descent action. The only candidate low frame and the approximately 100/30-m relative heights were fixed in advance. The agent arm had one reobservation action; the paired hold arm had zero. Both arms used the identical cached first Qwen response. The controller recorded each candidate answer, action, budget change, and stop reason. Reference answers were read only after the replay for scoring.

This is a MESSI-specific perception and decision adapter. The original T1 presence branch skips local descent, and MESSI supplies neither the disaster pre/post pair nor ChangeOS damage probabilities. Qwen instead supplied a YES/NO canopy answer and self-reported answer confidence; normalized binary entropy of that confidence replaced damage entropy. For the marked binary target, the adapter invoked the existing gate's damage-target utility formula and its 0.5 uncertainty trigger, 0.05 flight-cost weight, and 0.05 minimum utility. It stipulated that the marked region was visible, used zero horizontal recentering, and bypassed the damage-specific \texttt{RecheckController.assess}. The controller's answer, action-budget, trajectory, and stop paths remained in use with VLM answering and no search. This is an adapted offline decision test, not an unchanged T1 presence-policy or physical-flight test.

The hold arm answered 8/12 correctly, versus 10/12 for the action-enabled arm (Table~\ref{tab:messi-replay}); the two changed outcomes were corrections and none were harms. The agent requested the low image in all 12 episodes, and all episodes in both arms ended with \texttt{sufficient\_evidence}. Thus the replay verifies that the adapted controller can trigger, acquire, incorporate, and stop after a real lower-altitude image, but the observed operating point offers no evidence that it selectively suppresses unnecessary reobservations. Qwen's reported high-view confidence was at most 0.75 in every item, which made every item exceed the entropy trigger. These descriptive results do not estimate a stable accuracy gain.

\subsection{Expanded evaluation on public MESSI pairs}
\label{sec:messi-expanded}
We extended the case systematically after observing the 12-item results. The extension uses every annotation-consistent cell of a fixed 24-cell grid in five public MESSI descend sequences; \texttt{100\_0001} remains a pilot sequence. This yields 107 regions from 120 evaluation-sequence cells, with 13 ambiguous or cross-height discordant cells excluded before the extension's inference. We also paired each public 30-m frame in Agamim Paths A--C to its nearest 100-m frame by GPS and tested one fixed central region. Of 136 low frames, 120 passed distance and image-registration checks; 25 further pairs had ambiguous or discordant annotations, leaving 95 path pairs. The latter contain 83 positives and 12 negatives. The task is now \emph{visible vegetation including grass, trees, and shrubs}, matching MESSI's vegetation mask; it is distinct from the selected 12-item canopy task. Answers derive from agreement of high- and low-view masks, without visual confirmation of every region. The 202 units share frames and nearby ground content and must not be treated as independent flights.

The same high-repeat (A), high-source crop (B), and real low-image (C) branches completed all 202 vegetation items (Table~\ref{tab:messi-expanded}). On descend regions C answered 83/107 correctly versus B's 80/107, with seven C-over-B corrections and four harms. On path pairs C answered 83/95 versus B's 86/95, with one correction and four harms. Aggregated descriptively, both B and C answered 166/202 correctly; their eight corrections and eight harms cancel. The selected 12-item positive difference therefore does not extend to a consistent low-image advantage under this broader public-pair protocol.

\subsection{Full-release MESSI fixed observations and earlier Qwen replay}
\label{sec:messi-full}
After the public-sample extension, we obtained the author's full 2,525-image MESSI release and inventoried all image, annotation, and pose filenames. We applied the same vegetation task and two-height selection rules to every available descend sequence and to the Agamim and IrYamim paths with both 100- and 30-m acquisitions. The previously used \texttt{100\_0001} sequence remained pilot-only. One additional descend sequence failed the fixed 40-inlier registration criterion, and path pairs exceeding the prespecified 12-m GPS distance or failing annotation agreement were excluded. The Ha-Medinah Square path has only 60-m images, and the 50/70-m acquisitions do not enter this single-action protocol. The resulting 409 eligible regions comprise 280 descend-grid cells, 95 Agamim path pairs, and 34 IrYamim test-path pairs. The training paths replicate the earlier public-sample stratum; the newly available descend and test-path regions extend coverage. All selection and image hashes were fixed before the complete-release inference, with source and exclusion manifests retained separately from model inputs.

The original DisasterClaw presence gate explicitly answers from the wide view and never requests local descent. A diagnostic invoking the repository's \texttt{AgentVqaController} and that unmodified gate on all 409 MESSI items made zero rechecks and answered 287/409 first views correctly. It still used a MESSI VLM first-view adapter rather than the complete ChangeOS/hybrid disaster pipeline. This zero-action result establishes that a positive recheck claim on the vegetation question requires a declared task adapter. Its answer accuracy is not directly comparable with the token-entropy adapter, which uses a different first-answer prompt and scoring method.

In the full-release fixed-observation experiment, high-source crop B and real low-image C each answered 341/409 correctly (Table~\ref{tab:messi-full-fixed}); C corrected 13 B errors and harmed 13 B-correct answers. The descending sequences favored C by two answers, Agamim paths favored B by three, and IrYamim test paths favored C by one. Thus a real descent image did not consistently outperform digital enlargement across this eligible set.

\subsubsection{Single-temporal replay with DisasterClaw's legacy perception models}
\label{sec:messi-native-agent}
We then evaluated the repository's existing perception models and closed-loop controller on the same 409 eligible regions. The RescueNet YOLO checkpoint (six building-damage, vehicle, and water classes) has no vegetation category; we retain its detections as scene context and do not use them as vegetation evidence. Vegetation presence comes from the ADE20K SegFormer-B2 model, using tree (class 4), grass (9), plant (17), and palm (72). For a region, we map its mean soft vegetation occupancy $f$ to the task score $p=\mathrm{clip}[(f-0.005)/(0.05-0.005),0,1]$ and answer YES at $p\geq0.5$. This is a task-specific score mapping, not a calibration claim.

The model sees an unmarked registered ROI crop at each height. Each paired hold/agent run shares the exact high-view model output. The repository's \texttt{AgentVqaController} retains its evidence memory, answer and stop path, action budget, and trajectory; a narrow single-temporal adapter supplies the vegetation evidence and deterministic presence answer. The \texttt{RecheckController} retains its normalized two-class entropy gate; the 0.50 threshold is inherited from its existing default and was not fitted on MESSI. It is allowed one action. If it triggers, the offline environment releases the pre-registered 30-m crop and records a descend-only action matching the observed relative height difference. It does not execute horizontal recentering, search, or physical motion. The low image is not made available to either the perception callback or the controller before that action.

On the full 409-item set, the no-new-observation arm answered 369 correctly (90.2\%), and the agent arm answered 372 correctly (91.0\%). The controller requested 20 lower-view observations; six changed wrong answers to correct and three changed correct answers to wrong. By stratum, the agent changed 259/280 to 260/280 with 12 rechecks, 85/95 to 87/95 with seven, and 25/34 to 25/34 with one on the IrYamim test-path stratum (Table~\ref{tab:messi-native-agent}).

The 409-item hold arm makes only the first answer call. We therefore added a matched second-observation control for the exact 20 triggered items: after the shared high-view answer and identical controller trigger, one arm reprocessed the same high ROI while the other opened the registered low ROI. Each second-observation arm had the same controller state, one action, and two deterministic answer calls; only the image in the second call differed. The repeated-high arm answered 12/20 correctly, identical to its shared first answers, while the real-low arm answered 15/20 correctly (Table~\ref{tab:messi-trigger-control}).
