\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The benchmark contains 44 ROIs from three events, and questions from nearby regions may share image context. Although development and final questions use distinct tiles and building UIDs, their ROIs and rendered footprints can overlap geographically. Together with the fixed ChangeOS checkpoint's prior xBD exposure, these overlaps limit claims of spatial independence and transfer to unseen disasters. The final answer distribution is uneven, particularly for count questions. The image-aware pipelines substantially outperform the evaluated shortcut controls, but this result does not establish that every question requires visual evidence or exclude unmeasured shortcuts. Human review and deterministic annotation rules support reference construction without guaranteeing visual answerability for every question.

The camera ladder resamples orthographic imagery and uses asymmetric pre-/post-disaster processing. Unlike 3D UAV simulators that model vehicle dynamics and onboard sensing, it cannot reproduce changes in viewing angle, occlusion, acquisition conditions, or motion dynamics during physical descent. The platform therefore evaluates an action-conditioned observation interface, not flight control, sensor-resource scheduling under bandwidth or energy limits, or vehicle safety. The spatial uncertainty score is conditional on a selected predicted target and does not fully capture uncertainty about which damaged building is nearest. The target switch in Fig.~\ref{fig:help-harm-cases} illustrates this unresolved source of harm.

The initial MESSI case contains twelve manually selected canopy-presence regions and a one-answer C-versus-B advantage. The later vegetation extension includes grass and has a different question, so the cohorts are not pooled. Although its source is the complete 2,525-image release, only 409 regions meet the fixed 100/30-m pairing, registration, and cross-height mask-agreement rules. Thus the scored units are not all source images or independent flights. Excluding ambiguous masks and distant or unregistered path pairs can favor clear cases. Agamim paths are strongly positive-skewed, and nearby items share frames or ground content. Only 34 IrYamim test-path regions qualify, from one geographic path. Frame pairs and candidate low views are fixed offline; planar registration and the high-image bounding crop are approximate, and relative altitude is not local above-ground clearance. The real low view ties the enlarged high crop overall, with 13 corrections and 13 harms.

The full-release controller replay uses a declared single-temporal task adapter. The existing YOLO has no vegetation class and contributes context detections only; SegFormer ADE20K class probabilities supply vegetation occupancy. The occupancy-to-answer mapping and its binary entropy are uncalibrated, and the vegetation classes are not specialized for MESSI UAV imagery. The adapter replaces the disaster-damage perception and hybrid answer path with current-frame vegetation evidence and a deterministic presence answer; it does not run ChangeOS or a generative VLM. The repository's AgentVqaController evidence, budget, action, and stop loop and the RecheckController threshold state machine remain in use, with a generic vegetation evidence label added. Each lower view is fixed and registered in advance, and the replay records a descend-only action without executing flight or recentering. Across 409 items, the action-enabled arm gains three correct answers overall (six corrections and three harms). For the 20 triggered cases, the matched repeat-high control receives the same second-call budget as the real-low arm: repeat-high scores 12/20 and real-low 15/20, with nine correct and two wrong in both branches. The 34 IrYamim Test regions were previously included in Qwen-only exploratory runs; the legacy-model checkpoint, class mapping, and inherited 0.50 trigger were fixed before this replay, and no results from these 34 regions were used to tune them. We therefore do not treat IrYamim as an untouched holdout, and do not tune further on these items. These results establish an exercised offline decision loop on real multi-height imagery, not disaster-damage performance, autonomous view selection, physical flight feasibility, or a reliable benefit from descent.

Earlier full-release experiments used Qwen-only perception adapters and different decision signals, including a post hoc token-entropy threshold selected on pilot items. They remain exploratory and are reported separately from the legacy-model replay; their absolute accuracies cannot be compared as a controlled policy contrast because the observation interpretation and answer module differ. The fixed-observation B/C comparison also uses Qwen and should not be pooled with the YOLO/SegFormer controller outcomes.

A0\_NO\_RECHECK permits search, and policies may differ in subsequent answer-call counts. The matched-call diagnostic controls for an additional answer opportunity at recheck transitions but does not follow both branches to episode termination. Perception may remain nondeterministic under identical image pixels, and the 95\% ROI-cluster interval for the matched-call difference includes zero. The old-view agreement check varies prompts and seeds, so it does not exclude model variance under strictly identical calls. The qualitative examples illustrate observed mechanisms without establishing a generally positive recheck effect.

Recheck counts exclude search and do not capture total rendering, perception, generation, or flight costs. Of the 3,360 episodes, 42 lack a recorded state after the terminal fly action. This omission affects complete-motion summaries but leaves the terminal-answer denominator unchanged.
