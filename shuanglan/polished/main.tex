\title{DisasterClaw: A georeferenced imagery benchmark for task-dependent UAV disaster inspection}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\affiliation{organization={Anonymous submission}}
\begin{abstract}
Aerial disaster inspection requires balancing local detail against regional coverage. DisasterClaw evaluates this trade-off using paired, georeferenced xBD imagery. Each question retains a fixed geographic region and annotation-derived answer as simulated vehicle actions change the observation. Episode records link actions, predicted evidence, and answers. The human-reviewed final set contains 160 presence, damage, count, and spatial questions across 44 regions in three events. A development-fitted task-majority baseline achieves 57.50\% accuracy; text-only and permitted-metadata controls achieve 28.75\% and 36.25\%. At 161 recheck transitions selected by the task-conditioned policy T1, changed-view accuracy is 59.63\%, compared with 50.31\% for a fixed-view control with a matched answer call (difference: $+9.32$ percentage points; 95\% region-clustered interval: $[-2.00,+21.74]$). The paired outcomes include 36 corrections and 21 harms. Across complete episodes, T1 achieves 75.00\% accuracy, compared with 72.29\% for a search-enabled policy without dedicated rechecks; the adjusted difference interval includes zero. These results characterize task-dependent benefits and risks of reobservation, without establishing a consistently positive average benefit. DisasterClaw provides a traceable framework for measuring observation value within a controlled setting based on resampled orthographic imagery and a fixed perception backbone with prior xBD exposure.
\end{abstract}
