\title{DisasterClaw: A georeferenced imagery benchmark for task-dependent UAV disaster inspection}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\affiliation{organization={Anonymous submission}}
\begin{abstract}
Aerial disaster inspection poses a task-dependent sensing question: when does a changed view improve the requested answer, and when does it discard necessary context? DisasterClaw turns paired, georeferenced xBD imagery into an interactive inspection benchmark. Each question has a fixed geographic region and annotation-derived answer, while vehicle actions change the rendered observation; the runner records the resulting evidence and answer. The human-reviewed final set contains 160 presence, damage, count, and spatial questions over 44 regions in three events. A development-fitted task-majority baseline reaches 57.50\%, while text-only and permitted-runtime-metadata controls reach 28.75\% and 36.25\%; the image-aware no-dedicated-recheck operating point reaches 72.29\%. At 161 executed T1 recheck transitions, a fixed-view matched-call control is correct on 50.31\% and the changed-view branch on 59.63\% (paired difference $+9.32$ points; 95\% ROI-cluster interval $[-2.00,+21.74]$). The branches contain 36 wrong-to-correct and 21 correct-to-wrong transitions, showing both corrections and harms rather than a uniformly positive effect. Complete-policy results are consistent with this interpretation: T1 answers 360/480 question--repeat instances correctly (75.00\%), compared with 347/480 (72.29\%) for the search-enabled no-dedicated-recheck arm, whose adjusted interval spans zero. DisasterClaw therefore provides a traceable measurement framework for task-dependent observation value, not a demonstration of a universally superior recheck policy. The benchmark uses resampled orthographic imagery and a fixed perception backbone with prior xBD exposure.
\end{abstract}
