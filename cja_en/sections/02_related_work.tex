\section{Related work}
\subsection{Language-conditioned aerial autonomy}
Vision-and-language navigation connects instructions to sequential visual decisions~\cite{anderson2018vision}. In aerial environments, AerialVLN extends this task to UAV navigation, and CityNav emphasizes real-world aerial navigation data~\cite{liu2023aerialvln,lee2025citynav}. OpenFly includes a multi-engine simulation platform, a collection toolchain, and height-varying navigation trajectories~\cite{gao2026openfly}. Thus, altitude variation and language-conditioned aerial movement are established research directions. Our experiment focuses on the value of revisiting visual evidence for damage assessment, rather than proposing another navigation benchmark or comparing success rates across incompatible environments.

Large pretrained models can be composed into navigation systems without treating every component as a newly learned policy~\cite{shah2023lmnav}. CityNavAgent uses hierarchical semantic planning and global memory, while spatial representations have been studied specifically for language-model reasoning in aerial navigation~\cite{zhang2025citynavagent,gao2024stmr}. AerialClaw further develops modular aerial agents with skills and feedback~\cite{li2026aerialclaw}. These works constrain the novelty claimed for DisasterClaw: semantic planning, spatial memory, and tool execution provide implementation support. The scientific question examined here concerns the allocation and measured utility of additional observations.

\subsection{Active perception and decision value}
CityEQA directly connects urban exploration to question answering through a hierarchical agent~\cite{zhao2025cityeqa}. Its Collector adjusts the observation pose, and its evaluation examines answers across refinement steps. Active aerial question answering and the effects of view refinement therefore already have direct precedents. Our narrower focus is paired correction and harm under a common building-level observation budget, followed by a separate diagnosis of observation use in disaster questions. The different environments and scoring protocols preclude a direct performance ranking against CityEQA.

Active perception treats sensing as an activity that can be controlled to serve a task~\cite{bajcsy1988active}. More recent planning under partial observability combines information gathering with uncertainty about states and action outcomes~\cite{curtis2024tampura}. This literature motivates evaluating an observation by the decision it enables, rather than by visual change alone. The present study uses a simpler setting: two-dimensional source imagery, a finite camera ladder, and short inspection episodes. It evaluates heuristic observation rules and their failure cases; it does not implement a general belief-space planner.

Entropy reduction is a convenient signal for these rules, but it is not the same quantity as expected task utility. A probability distribution can become more concentrated around an incorrect class. Conversely, an observation may improve a count or spatial answer without changing the most uncertain building's label. We therefore retain separate endpoints for classification, correction and harm, final question correctness, and executed observation count. The distinction is operational, and does not require a theorem equating prediction flips with all possible forms of observation value.

\subsection{Disaster interpretation and uncertainty}
xBD provides paired satellite imagery and building-damage annotations for disaster assessment~\cite{gupta2019xbd}. RescueADI studies autonomous agents for adaptive interpretation of remote-sensing disaster images~\cite{liu2024rescueadi}. These resources and systems establish that both damage recognition and tool-based disaster analysis predate this work. Our setting adds a controlled change in rendered camera footprint and asks whether the associated evidence changes justify a limited sensing budget. Because the source images are satellite products, this is an emulation of aerial inspection rather than a collection of physical UAV revisits.

Temperature scaling addresses probability calibration, while evaluation under dataset shift shows why in-distribution calibration should not be treated as a universal reliability property~\cite{guo2017calibration,ovadia2019can}. Selective prediction measures the trade-off between coverage and error, and uncertainty-aware language-model planning has also been studied through help-seeking behavior~\cite{geifman2019selectivenet,ren2023knowno}. We use these distinctions to separate calibrated detector probabilities, prediction-set triggers, and the VLM's answer confidence. A calibration temperature does not establish a guarantee for the composed agent, and a prediction-set size used as a sensing score does not establish coverage under the present event shift and adaptive observation process.
