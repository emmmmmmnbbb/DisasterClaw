\section{Conclusion}
DisasterClaw turns georeferenced pre- and post-disaster imagery into traceable closed-loop inspection episodes. A fixed binary ChangeOS detector, simulated observation control, task-conditioned action gate, evidence memory, and question-answering interface make it possible to follow a sensing decision from movement through perception to the terminal answer.

In 160 human-reviewed final questions over three generation repeats, T1\_TASK reaches 75.00\% accuracy versus 72.29\% for holding and executes fewer reobservations than the other active policies. This is an observed improvement in the tested sample, not an established stable advantage over no reobservation: the prespecified adjusted interval for the 2.71-point difference spans zero. The always-reobserve policy uses 923 actions and finishes at 63.96\%, illustrating that more observation can also be harmful. Together with the development camera-ladder's corrections and harms, these results support the platform's role in measuring task-dependent observation value.

The next scientific steps are a randomized common-budget comparison, fuller oracle decomposition, component-level timing, independently reviewed questions across more events, and a perception checkpoint evaluated without event exposure. Claims about real flight additionally require external action and imaging validation. Until then, the contribution is an imagery-grounded environment and a measured policy comparison with explicitly bounded evidence, not a demonstrated general-purpose flight policy.
