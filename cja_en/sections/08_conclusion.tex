\section{Conclusion}
DisasterClaw fixes the geographic scope and answer of a disaster-inspection question while allowing the observation to change. Its episode record connects actions, predicted building evidence, answer calls, and terminal task outcomes. The four task families expose a tension between local detail and regional coverage that a building classifier score alone cannot show.

On the frozen final set, text-only and permitted-metadata shortcuts remain well below image-aware operating points. At matched recheck transitions, changed observations produce both more wrong-to-correct than correct-to-wrong answers and a positive point estimate over a fixed-view matched call, while the ROI-cluster interval still includes zero. Task-wise patterns are consistent with a local-detail versus regional-coverage trade-off: damage and count transitions improve numerically, whereas spatial transitions worsen. T1\_TASK has the highest observed complete-policy accuracy, but its adjusted contrast with the search-enabled A0\_NO\_RECHECK arm spans zero. The contribution is therefore a benchmark interface and a bounded, traceable measurement of observation value, rather than a demonstrated universal observation policy.
