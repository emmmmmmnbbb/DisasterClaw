\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
\subsection{Inspection task and observation model}
A task consists of a question $q$, a region of interest (ROI), and a reference answer $y_q$. A single UAV has an observation state $s_t=(x_t,y_t,h_t)$ describing its map position and height. Its image is $I_t=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M},s_t)$, where $\mathcal{M}$ is the available georeferenced imagery and $\mathcal{R}$ crops and resamples a ground footprint. The notation describes the implemented observation state, not a complete aircraft dynamics model. Question types cover building damage, damage counts, presence of severe damage, and spatial relations.

For a nadir-view camera with horizontal field of view $\theta$ and output width $W$, the simulated footprint width $L$ and effective ground sampling distance $g$ are
\begin{equation}
 L(h)=2h\tan(\theta/2),\qquad g(h)=\frac{L(h)}{W}.
 \label{eq:geometry}
\end{equation}
We use $\theta=60^\circ$, $W=1024$, and an assumed source scale of $0.5$ m/pixel. A source tile therefore spans 512 m. Footprints of three, two, and one tile widths give cruise, intermediate, and floor views at approximately 1330.2, 886.8, and 443.4 m, with effective GSDs of 1.5, 1.0, and 0.5 m/pixel. The heights follow from the chosen camera parameters and source-scale assumption. They do not validate that scale for every source image or establish aircraft operating limits.

Shrinking the footprint increases the pixels assigned to the target ROI while removing surrounding context. At the floor, the image reaches the assumed source resolution; further physical detail cannot be recovered from this imagery. The renderer does not model three-dimensional occlusion, new viewing angles, atmospheric effects, motion blur, or changes between real acquisition times. \subsection{Budget and task endpoints}
In the offline experiment, $N$ buildings each have a cruise probability vector $p_i^c$ and a floor vector $p_i^f$ over four damage classes. For budget fraction $b$, a policy selects a set $S_b$ of $k=\operatorname{round}(bN)$ buildings. The evaluated vector is
\begin{equation}
 p_i^{(b)}=\begin{cases}p_i^f,&i\in S_b,\\p_i^c,&i\notin S_b.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
Each selected building counts as one additional observation in this abstraction. This accounting does not represent a route that physically visits every selected building, nor does it price the distance between them.

Let $\hat y_i^v=\arg\max_j p_{ij}^v$. A correctable case has $\hat y_i^c\ne y_i$ and $\hat y_i^f=y_i$; a harmful case has $\hat y_i^c=y_i$ and $\hat y_i^f\ne y_i$. For a fixed selected set, the accuracy difference is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\mathrm{Acc}(S_b)=\frac{N_{\mathrm{corrected}}(S_b)-N_{\mathrm{harmed}}(S_b)}{N}.
 \label{eq:net}
\end{equation}
This accounting identity applies to the paired hard-label endpoint. It is not a characterization of macro-F1, probability quality, or language-task utility. An unchanged predicted label may still accompany changes in those quantities.

Online, the agent can search, reobserve by centering or descending, answer, or abstain, subject to configured episode limits. We evaluate question correctness over all questions, counting abstentions as unsuccessful answers, and report actual reobservation actions separately. If $a_q$ is the final answer, the task score is $|Q|^{-1}\sum_q\mathbf{1}[a_q=y_q]$. The purpose is to assess whether extra sensing changes this endpoint and where its cost is incurred. No optimization guarantee is assumed for the implemented controller.
