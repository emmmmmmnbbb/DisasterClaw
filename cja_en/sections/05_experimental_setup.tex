\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Data populations and exposure boundary}
The development population contains 2,921 buildings from 20 ROIs in Hurricane Harvey and the Mexico earthquake. The principal evaluation population contains 3,753 unique buildings from 44 ROIs: 1,303 buildings in Hurricane Michael, 1,448 in the Moore tornado, and 1,002 in Nepal flooding. There are 15, 15, and 14 evaluation ROIs in these events, respectively. The development and evaluation event sets are disjoint for fitting the allocation statistics. This split does not imply an event-disjoint perception model: the frozen manifest explicitly marks the \texttt{xview2\_first} backbone as having prior evaluation-event exposure. All principal offline and online results are interpreted within that boundary.

The online population contains 160 unique questions across the same 44 evaluation ROIs, with 58, 47, and 55 questions from the three events, respectively. The question-type counts are 44 presence, 43 damage, 43 count, and 30 spatial questions. Ten configurations were evaluated on an identical set of question IDs, producing 1,600 records without duplicate configuration/question pairs. These are repeated evaluations of 160 questions, not 1,600 independent tasks. Building predictions and question records have different units and cannot be pooled into one sample size.

The archived results permit arithmetic reconstruction from predictions, labels, and correctness flags. The source image collection, frozen question-set entities, and exact training data are not all available in the current reproduction package. Thus, reconstruction verifies the reported arithmetic and local file consistency, rather than independently certifying annotations, checkpoint exposure, or the original scoring implementation. The outstanding requirements are stated in \ref{app:provenance}.

\subsection{Configurations and recorded execution}
The frozen calibration parameters are $T=3.5635948726$ and APS threshold $\hat q=0.8064657026$, with nominal calibration parameter $\alpha=0.1$. The temperature was selected on the development cruise probabilities by minimizing negative log-likelihood over 241 logarithmically spaced candidates in $[0.25,4]$. The offline allocation budgets are $b\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1\}$; $b=0.25$ is the frozen reference operating point. We reproduce all seven policy curves, retaining the label-informed diagnostic only for mechanism interpretation.

The online configurations comprise hold, random allocation, fixed allocation, raw entropy, calibrated entropy, APS triggering, expected entropy reduction, and three motion variants. The nominal limits allow six search actions and up to two reobservations per question, with hold allowing no reobservation. Random and fixed controls were intended to derive budgets from the expected-entropy reference. The three motion variants allow centering, descent, or combined motion. Their actual costs are reported alongside correctness because the archived budget check fails: intended matching and executed matching differ.

The online runs use seed 42 and two shards. Their manifests record Python 3.11.15, NumPy 2.4.4, PyTorch 2.11, and Transformers 5.5.4, with zero reported execution errors. Both point to the same frozen manifest and question-set hash. However, they identify a dirty source tree, and the model-identity fields are empty. The software versions are recorded execution metadata, not a validated environment reconstruction. \authorquery{Supply the exact VLM provider, model/version, decoding parameters, archived prompt, and the uncommitted source snapshot used for these runs. \subsection{Metrics and statistical scope}
For the building endpoint we report accuracy, unweighted macro-F1 over four classes, multiclass Brier score, negative log-likelihood (NLL), and expected calibration error (ECE) using 15 equal-width confidence bins. Brier score is the mean sum of squared differences from the one-hot label, without division by the number of classes. Corrected and harmed counts follow Eq.~\eqref{eq:net}. These metrics are computed on the same building population, including unmatched cases under the archived convention.

We add an exploratory paired ROI bootstrap for two comparisons at $b=0.25$: calibrated entropy versus random allocation, and calibrated versus raw entropy. Each of 2,000 resamples draws ROIs with replacement within each observed event, preserving that event's ROI count. We aggregate the sampled confusion matrices before recomputing macro-F1 differences. The seed is 20260905; the reported limits are the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Allocation masks, fitted parameters, and the observed event mixture are held fixed. These intervals address sampling variation among ROIs within the three events, not training uncertainty, repeated random-policy seeds, or transfer to new disasters. The analysis was added after inspecting the records and is reported as exploratory.

Online accuracy counts every question in its denominator. We also report gain and loss relative to hold on paired question IDs, actual additional observation counts, and the number of episodes receiving them. Differences between configurations may include generation variability and unmatched exposure to sensing; they are not automatically caused by motion. For confidence analysis, we evaluate non-abstained answers at fixed retrospective thresholds and retain all answers tied at each threshold. Coverage is accepted questions divided by 160, and risk is the error fraction among accepted questions. No threshold is fitted or deployed by this analysis.

Motion counts are the principal online cost measure. The implementation also estimates movement time from geometric displacement and configured speeds. It lacks a validated aircraft energy model, realistic battery consumption, and aerodynamic constraints. We therefore do not interpret this time estimate as measured flight endurance or use it to claim energy efficiency, safety certification, or fixed-wing feasibility.
